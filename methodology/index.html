Agentic Readiness Score methodology

How every rating is produced: the questions, the repeat runs, the web search comparison, the five parts, the weighting and the bands. Published so results can be checked and cited.

Summary

Each rating is based on 19 customer questions, each asked 5 times, with AI web search on and off. Results are combined with checks of the business’s website and data across five parts, weighted for the type of business, and reported as a score from 0 to 100 in five bands.

The question set

We ask a fixed set of 19 questions, written the way customers talk to AI assistants. They follow three stages of a purchase:

StageWhat the customer is doingExample form
DiscoveryWorking out what they need and where to start“I need help with X. Where do I start?”
EvaluationChecking out a specific business“Tell me about [business].”
ChoosingDeciding who to buy from“Who’s the best [service] in [place]?”

Questions are adapted to the business’s category and location. The same set is used for every business in a comparison, so results are like for like.

Repeat runs

AI assistants don’t give the same answer twice. A single answer is a yes or no that could be luck either way. Asking each question five times turns it into a mention probability: “named in 4 of 5 runs” is 0.8.

Across 19 questions that gives 95 runs per rating, each answered with web search on and again with it off. Five runs is the standard for published index results; more runs give tighter estimates at higher cost.

Web search on and off

Every question is asked with the assistant’s web search enabled and again with it disabled. With search on, the assistant can look the business up in the moment. With search off, it relies on what it learned in training.

The two mention rates are compared as the confidence ratio:

Confidence ratio = mention rate with web search ÷ mention rate without web search

RatioReading
1.5 or belowStrong. The business is part of the assistant’s knowledge.
Above 1.5, up to 3Moderate. Known, but partly relies on being found by search.
Above 3Weak. Mainly found through search.
No mentions without searchSearch-dependent. Not named from memory at all.

The five parts

The score combines five parts, each scored from 0 to 100:

  1. Technical access. Whether AI crawlers can reach and read the website.
  2. Content clarity. Whether content can be understood, quoted and summarised accurately.
  3. Brand command. How often assistants name the business, and with what sentiment, across the question set.
  4. Structured data. Whether products, prices, services and business details are machine-readable.
  5. Transaction readiness. Whether an AI agent could enquire, book or buy.

Brand command is built from the repeat runs above: the mention rate across all answers, and the share of mentions that are positive.

Business-type weighting

What makes a business ready for AI agents depends on what it sells. The overall score uses one of six weighting profiles: e-commerce, professional services, local business, software and technology, media and publishing, and a general profile where every part counts equally.

For example, e-commerce gives more weight to structured data and transaction readiness, while professional services gives more weight to brand command. The profile used is stated in every report, and businesses are compared with others on the same profile. Profile weights are reviewed periodically; any change is recorded in the version history below.

Bands

  • Leading 80–100A default recommendation in its category.
  • Visible 60–79Regularly named and described accurately.
  • Emerging 40–59Known, but often passed over.
  • Low visibility 20–39Occasionally named, rarely recommended.
  • Invisible 0–19AI assistants don’t know it exists.

Limitations

  • Ratings are snapshots. AI models and the web change. Every rating carries its test date and should be cited with it.
  • Assistants differ. Results apply to the assistants tested, which every report lists. They don’t predict every AI product.
  • Questions shape answers. A fixed question set makes comparisons fair, but it can’t cover every way customers ask.
  • Sampling error. Five runs per question gives a reliable estimate, not an exact figure. Small differences between businesses should not be over-read.

Glossary

Agentic readiness
How prepared a business is to be found, understood, recommended and used by AI agents.
Brand command
How often, and how favourably, AI assistants mention a business.
Mention probability
The share of answers that name the business, from 0 to 1.
Confidence ratio
Mention rate with web search divided by mention rate without it.
Snapshot
A dated record of a business’s results.
